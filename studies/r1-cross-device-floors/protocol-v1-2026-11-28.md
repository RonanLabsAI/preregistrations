# Locked spec: R1, noise floors across machines

Drafted 2026-11-28 ~07:00 PT, before any R1 output exists; revision r2 ~08:00 PT after the literature read, the
Cortex consult and an adversarial review (Grok; Gemini's quota was exhausted for ~5 days; review text in
`docs/reviews/2026-11-28-grok-r1-card.md`). Status: **r3, approved by Stephen 2026-11-28 ~10:35 PT ("approve as is"; Spark 1 approved, after S0's Spark
units; RTX 5080 approved from midnight 2026-11-28/29); stage card frozen 11:02 PT**. Frozen by `scripts/r1/freeze_r1.py --stage card` after those. Stephen approved drafting it
2026-11-28 ("would def improve paper 2 and give a signif upgrade on their paper").

**Why.** Paper 2 (determinism note, draft) found greedy reruns on one GB300 server byte-identical for 32.0% of
MMLU-Pro and 26.8% of MATH-500 items, verdict-level flips of 6.8-8.8%, and batch-invariant byte parity across a GB300
and two GB10 boards. Rane (arXiv 2611.29663, 22 Nov 2026) published the closer-controlled version on one consumer GPU
(RTX 5070 Ti, Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507, vLLM 0.33.0 offline `generate`, 3,380 items): restart floor 1.38% graded flips,
reordering 1.72%, exact serving features inside the floor, FP8 KV 3.36%, batch invariance bitwise across restarts and
reorderings, and single replica pairs varying 2.5x; their stated limit is one GPU ("on another GPU would not be
exact there"). R1 asks what neither paper answers: **does a noise floor measured on one device hold on another device**, and
does batch-invariant byte identity hold across devices, with LoRA adapters, and at what cost on Blackwell parts.

## Prior work read in full before drafting (2026-11-28; texts in the session scratchpad, not committed)
- Rane 2611.29663: restart/reorder floors, exact features, BI bitwise on one RTX 5070 Ti (above).
- Blagojevich & Sunderland 2611.15671 (one GB10, Qwen3.5 9B/27B decision tasks, single forward passes): vLLM restarts
  flip 0-22 of 2,400 decisions; a third instance shows the floor tracked a cold vs warm compile cache (22 vs 4 flips),
  "a single repeat can understate the floor by more than five times"; "one device ... a property of this device".
  Cites vllm#58899: greedy output differs between fresh processes "also with VLLM_BATCH_INVARIANT=1" (autotuned fused
  kernel). R1 adopts: >= 3 instances, the largest pair reported, Clopper-Pearson for zero-flip pairs, one shared
  bootstrap matrix, flips split worse / better / wrong-to-wrong, cold vs warm compile cache as an explicit factor.
- Pape et al. 2605.19537 (NeurIPS 2026; 5 engines, H100/L40): backend alone moves scores up to 16.6 points, mostly
  hidden defaults (BOS, repetition penalty); no floor, no concurrency, no BI. R1 adopts: a hidden-defaults audit
  (effective sampling params and generation_config as applied, BOS handling) logged per run.
- BlockLock 2611.06977 (A6000/A100/H100): vLLM BI "more than a 2x latency penalty"; bitwise via full-vocab logit
  hashes; cross-architecture "outside our scope". Conflicts with Rane's ~4% BI cost: R1's per-chip cost arm settles it.
- Patodiya 2609.04748 (RTX 4090, BFCL episodes): no prefix reuse -> every token identical; vLLM fresh server with cache
  on 0/80 episodes diverge, both passes from one process 7/80 and 22/80; vLLM leaves cached-token fields null (use
  engine logs). R1 adopts: verify prefix caching from engine counters; same-process x prefix-cache interaction.
- Zhang et al. 2510.22876 (Table 4, Qwen3-4B, vLLM v1): BI restores parity on H100 (80/80) but not on A100 (62/80):
  BI is not uniform across chips, which R1's transfer question tests directly on Blackwell parts.
- DriftBench (Vitale, MLSys 2026; abstract, slides, repo only; paper 403): hardware/precision/framework drift over
  105 configs with no same-config floor in the released data, so its "hardware drift" includes run-to-run noise.

- Cortex consult (ronanlabs-hq docs/research/2026-11-26-program/cortex-consult-r1.md, 109 queries, 47 ids verified):
  Vega-Fafnir 2611.27554 (speculative verification changes 60% of Qwen3-0.6B greedy outputs, every first divergence at

- **Web and vLLM-tracker novelty check (2026-11-28, read-only; re-verify the cited PRs at freeze).** LoRA under BI:

- **S0 (RonanLabsAI/preregistrations PR #8, merged 2026-11-28 04:31 PT; protocol

**What is still new for R1 (ranked, after review):** (1) cross-device transfer of a same-config floor, and of
batch-invariant byte identity, on four named Blackwell devices; (2) interleaved multi-LoRA batches vs single-adapter runs
under batch-invariant kernels, byte for byte and across restarts (single-adapter repeatability is a replication of
vLLM #70061/#70406). Descriptive additions: output-budget truncation, batch-invariant throughput and latency per device, serving
path (HTTP concurrency vs offline) against a floor, same-process x prefix caching. The restart + shuffle protocol is a
replication (Rane, Blagojevich): infrastructure, not a claim. Model size is a covariate. **Not claimed:** a
hardware-class law (one board per SKU), an explanation of paper 2's larger flip rates beyond the matched arm below,
settling BlockLock's latency figure (older architectures, a different metric), or cross-chip non-uniformity of BI as
such (Zhang et al. showed H100 != A100).

## Questions
1. **Transfer (confirmatory).** With scheduling matched, is the graded-outcome flip rate between two devices larger
   than each device's own warm-restart floor?
2. **Batch-invariant identity (pass/fail).** Are batch-invariant outputs byte-identical across every device pair and
   across cold and warm restarts?
3. **Floor structure (descriptive, interval rules).** Same-process vs restart (cold-warm, warm-warm) vs reordered.
4. **Matched comparison with Rane (descriptive).** Rane's model, path and caps on a consumer RTX 5080 and on Station
   GPUs.
5. **Serving-path screen (descriptive).** HTTP concurrency 96 and 1, prefix caching, output budget, FP8 KV.
6. **Adapters.** Batch-invariant identity with merged checkpoints, single LoRA modules, and interleaved LoRA batches.
7. **Cost (descriptive).** Batch-invariant throughput and latency on the GB300 and RTX PRO 6000.
8. **Set size (diagnostic).** Rerun SE against n.

## Design
**Scheduler-matched transfer arm (primary).** Offline `LLM.generate` in the container (Rane's path), all prompts
submitted at once in one fixed order, identical engine caps on every device: `max-num-seqs 32`,
`max-num-batched-tokens 8192`, chunked prefill on, prefix caching off, CUDA graphs on, KV dtype bf16, and
`num-gpu-blocks-override` set to one value every device can hold (so KV capacity, and therefore scheduling and
preemption, are the same); the realized maximum concurrent sequences and preemption count are read from the engine
and must match across devices, else the run is discarded. Logprobs off in primary runs.
Per device, model Qwen3-4B, mode batch-invariant off and on: **5 fresh restarts** (container and process new): 2 cold
(empty compile/autotune caches) and 3 warm, plus 1 same-process rerun and 2 seeded order permutations.
**Matched Rane arm.** Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507, offline path, Rane's caps (GSM8K 768, MATH500 4,096, others 1,024) and,
if released, his items; batch-invariant off, 3 warm restarts, on the RTX 5080, the RTX PRO 6000 and the GB300.
**Serving-path screen** (GB300, the paper 2 device; Qwen3-1.7B; batch-invariant off; 3 warm restarts each; one factor
changed from paper 2's reference = HTTP server at concurrency 96): HTTP concurrency 1; offline generate; prefix caching
on; budget 768 vs 4,096 (flips split into items hitting the cap in either run vs items completed in both); FP8 KV (gated:
accuracy within 2 points of bf16 before its flips are read). A screen: no attribution of "largest contributor" unless
intervals separate.
**Adapter arm** (RTX PRO 6000; Qwen3-1.7B with paper 3's pt17 sql/tools/code adapters; the same 2,244 transfer-core
items for base and adapters; batch-invariant on; 3 warm restarts each): merged checkpoint; single LoRA module;
interleaved batch of one adapter repeated (max-loras 3, all requests to one adapter); interleaved mixed adapters
(requests assigned round-robin by item id).
**Cost arm** (GB300 and RTX PRO 6000; Qwen3-4B; batch-invariant on vs off): steady-state throughput at concurrency 32
with requests refilled continuously for 10 minutes (first and last minute excluded), and single-request prefill and
decode latency on 50 prompts; CUDA-graph capture logged in both modes.
**Logprob smoke.** 100 items with logprobs (top-20) on vs off must be byte-identical before any margin data is
collected; if identical, margins are collected on the second same-process rerun only; if not, margins come from a
separate run, never from primary runs.

## Metrics and analysis (`scripts/r1/r1_analysis.py`, hashed before first run)
- Per item and run pair: graded-outcome flip (primary endpoint), extracted-answer flip, text flip (descriptive).
- Floors, separately: warm-warm, cold-warm, cold-cold, same-process, order permutation vs canonical. Per-item flip
  probability over the condition's run pairs; floor = equal-benchmark-weight mean.
- Paired differences use one shared bootstrap resample matrix (2,000 resamples, clusters = MMLU-Pro category, else
  benchmark, rng 0). Intervals are item-sampling error conditional on the runs; this is stated.
- Zero-flip rates: cluster-bootstrap upper bound (Clopper-Pearson shown for reference). The largest pair per
  condition is reported without an interval.
- Churn beside net accuracy; flips split worse / better / wrong-to-wrong; exact McNemar on lost/gained as a direction
  check, never as the flip test.
- Set size: SE of the rerun score difference from subsamples (n = 100 to 6,016) against sqrt(D)/n (D = discordant
  items; the paired-difference SE) and, for a single score, sqrt(p(1-p)/n); a diagnostic.
- Rescore check: every stored output rescored twice; differing rows reported (IFEval global-RNG bug pinned and stated).
- statistics reuse evalci (2607.04429) where it covers a computation.

## Hypotheses and predictions (committed before any R1 output)
**Confirmatory (one test).** H1a: on the pre-specified pair GB300 vs RTX PRO 6000 (stratum S), Qwen3-4B, batch-invariant
off, scheduler-matched arm, warm restarts: the cross-device graded flip rate minus the mean of the two devices'
warm-warm floors is > 0, 95% CI excluding 0. Prediction: > 0 (the floor does not transfer), with the excess about the
size of the floor itself. If the within-device floor's CI upper bound is below 0.5%, only the difference is reported,
not a ratio. **H1b (pass/fail, every pair, no exceptions):** batch-invariant outputs byte-identical across all six device
pairs and across cold and warm restarts within each device, for Qwen3-4B; one mismatching item fails that pair.
Prediction: passes within each device (cold and warm, if the pin includes the #58899 fix; else cold-warm pairs are the
risk) and among the three sm12x devices (GB10, RTX PRO 6000, RTX 5080: one tuned tile table); GB300 (sm10x table) vs
the sm12x devices is genuinely uncertain (paper 2 saw GB300-GB10 parity on an older pin). Failures are not excused.
**Descriptive, with interval rules (no multiplicity adjustment, no confirmatory language):**
- H2: restart - same-process > 0 and reorder - restart > 0 each "supported" only if its interval excludes 0, else
  "inconclusive"; cold-warm vs warm-warm reported.
- Matched Rane arm: the restart floor on the RTX 5080, reported beside Rane's 1.38% with the item/prompt differences.
- H5 (adapters): each serving path (merged, single module, interleaved-same, interleaved-mixed) byte-identical with
  itself across restarts (pass/fail per path); interleaved-mixed vs single-module and interleaved-same vs single-module
  byte parity per item (rates, the new part); merged vs module reported as a rate, predicted unequal by construction
  (#66651, TRL #8632), not claimed identical.
- H6 (cost): batch-invariant throughput loss estimated per device; called "closer to Rane than to BlockLock" only if the
  loss is < 50% on both devices; latency reported beside it.
- Serving-path screen and set size: estimates only.

## Budget (estimate; re-projected at the first freeze from a timing smoke)
Transfer arm (4 devices x 2 modes x 8 runs x 2,244 items, 4B): GB300 ~2.5 h, RTX PRO 6000 ~3 h, GB10 ~9 h, RTX 5080
~6 h. Matched Rane arm ~3 h. Screen (GB300, 1.7B) ~2 h. Adapter arm ~2 h. Cost arm ~1.5 h. Smokes ~1 h. ~30 GPU-h; cap
30. Drop order if over: GB10 order permutations, then the screen's FP8 KV and budget factors, then the full 7,260-item
set (keep the transfer core). Never dropped: the RTX 5080 transfer and matched arms, the confirmatory pair.

## Smoke and stop rules
- Timing smoke (100 items per device) and the logprob smoke before the grid; re-project the budget.
- Scheduler-match check: realized concurrency and preemptions equal across devices in the transfer arm, else discard
  and fix the caps (logged as a deviation).
- Batch-invariant self-check per device on the smoke items is reported but does not excuse H1b.
- Any run whose resolved engine config, backend or graph capture differs from the declared one is discarded and rerun.

## What this does not show
Other serving engines (SGLang, TensorRT-LLM), non-NVIDIA hardware, sampled decoding, models above 4B in the
confirmatory test, MoE models (2511.07585), speculative decoding (excluded; 2611.27554), more than one board per SKU,
production fleets.
