# S0: byte parity between DGX Spark (GB10) and RTX PRO 6000 under batch-invariant vLLM

Study: S0, line B (reproducibility of LLM evals across hardware). Mission Control products
`s0-spark-6000-parity` (RTX PRO 6000 units) and `s0-spark-6000-parity-spark1` (DGX Spark units). They are
one study under one protocol; the queue needs one product per hardware class.
Frozen: 2026-11-28 (PT). Nothing has run under this protocol yet.
Status: draft for review; execution follows the public commit of this file and the PI's approval.

## Question

With vLLM's batch-invariant mode on and identical serving flags, does a DGX Spark (GB10, sm_121) produce
byte-identical greedy outputs to an RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Max-Q (sm_120)? Same model, prompts and request
body on both. We ask this for three models from two families: Qwen3-1.7B, and IBM Granite 4.1 3B and 8B.

Before the hardware comparison can mean anything, each device must reproduce itself, and its outputs must
not depend on batch composition. Both are measured here too.

## Decision

The program forbids mixing Spark and RTX PRO 6000 outputs in one locked result until this check passes.
Paper 3 v2 (V1, Granite 4.1 3B and 8B) and the reproducibility audit (R1) wait on it. The verdict is per
model:

- PASS: V1 and R1 may pool Spark and RTX PRO 6000 outputs for that model in a locked result. This applies
  only under the serving configuration (fixed KV pool) and request body frozen here. Outputs served with
  V1's current flags (memory utilization 0.85, no fixed pool) are not covered. The one comparison of those
  flags with the fixed pool is reported for V1 to weigh, but it licenses nothing.
- NONDETERMINISTIC: a device does not reproduce itself under identical settings. No pooling.
- BATCH-VARIANT: on either device, outputs change with client concurrency. No pooling.
- HARDWARE-DIFFERENT: each device reproduces itself and its outputs do not change between the two
  concurrencies tested on it, but the two devices disagree. No pooling for that model. V1 scores it on the
  RTX PRO 6000 only. R1 treats hardware as a factor for it.
- UNINFORMATIVE: every comparison above is identical, but the batch-invariant-off control also shows no
  mismatch, so this prompt set cannot expose batch noise for that model on this stack. No pooling license;
  the identity counts are still reported.
- INCOMPLETE: a required unit is missing or failed a treatment, schema or completeness check. No pooling
  until rerun under a dated amendment.

Finding the cause of any non-PASS verdict (attention kernel, matmul path, BlockLock) would be a new study.

A PASS licenses pooling of outputs produced under this protocol's configuration on these two platforms.
It says nothing about outputs from earlier runs, including the determinism note's October runs, whose server
flags were not logged. For Qwen3-1.7B, a PASS extends the earlier finding (one GB300 and two DGX Sparks
byte-identical on these 1,179 prompts) to the GB10 / RTX PRO 6000 pair. For each Granite model, a PASS is a first check of a second family
across these two chips. A HARDWARE-DIFFERENT verdict is a receipted case of two devices that each pass the
same within-device checks and still disagree.

## Prior work

- Our determinism note (RonanLabs, draft, 2026-11-14): with `VLLM_BATCH_INVARIANT=1`, Qwen3-1.7B greedy
  outputs were byte-identical on 1,179/1,179 prompts across one GB300 and two DGX Sparks. Its stated
  limitations include that the RTX PRO 6000 and a second family were not tested.
- BlockLock (arXiv 2611.06977): bitwise reproducibility when companion requests change, within one
  deployment. It tests A6000, A100 and H100 separately and makes no claim across GPU models. Its families
  include Qwen3, Llama 3.1 and Gemma 3; Granite is not among them.
- Exact on Paper (2611.29663): batch-invariant kernels reproduce exact serving features byte for byte on one
  4B model in vLLM, across restarts and reordering. Hardware is not varied.
- 2506.09501: greedy bf16 outputs change with GPU type, count and batch size without invariant kernels.
- 2606.00279: bit-exact recomputation across NVIDIA GPU variants by software emulation, not native serving.
- 2611.13730: bit-identical outputs across CPU instruction sets and CPU/GPU with exact integer arithmetic,
  not the floating-point path.
- 2511.17826: identical outputs across tensor-parallel sizes with tree-based invariant kernels.
- 2606.08051: across 14 hosted endpoints, mean F1 barely moved, but one family (Aya) lost 2.7 to 5.1 F1
  points on another serving stack. That is a score shift, not a byte-identity measure. It is the reason each
  family gets its own check here.

We found no paper that reports native floating-point byte identity between GPU architectures under vLLM's
batch-invariant mode. None covers the GB10 / RTX PRO 6000 pair or Granite.

## Why the outcome is not settled in advance

In the pinned vLLM build (`vllm/model_executor/determinism/batch_invariant.py`,
`enable_batch_invariant_mode`; `batch_invariant_configs.py`, `_get_tuned_matmul_arch_family`), batch-invariant
mode does three things on Blackwell. It disables split-K in cuBLASLt through its workspace settings. It
replaces softmax, log-softmax, mean and bmm with fixed-order Triton kernels. It picks Triton matmul tile
configurations by GPU family. GB10 (compute capability 12.1) and the RTX PRO 6000 (12.0) both map to the
`sm120` family and the same shared-memory branch. They differ in SM count, and cuBLASLt may choose
different algorithms per device. vLLM documents batch invariance as a property within one device and does
not promise identity across devices.

All three models are dense decoder-only transformers (attention plus MLP; Granite 4.1 here is
`GraniteForCausalLM`, with no state-space layers). Every matmul, norm, softmax and attention call therefore
goes through the paths above.

  `46cf80d26b7755c335fc3f0d7e008e9e8085830af858408b903eab200f8a7bdc`.

- RTX PRO 6000: `NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Max-Q Workstation Edition`, compute capability 12.0, 96 GB,

## Serving (identical on both hardware classes except where stated)

- Image: `vllm/vllm-openai@sha256:3af6a45b060d681aa6254200f87859d1bb3c1f962afb3d15540b4b31d4a41063`, an
  arm64 image (vLLM 0.30.1rc1.dev245+gaf7f9488c, torch 2.13.0+cu130, transformers 5.17.0, triton 3.7.1,
  flashinfer 0.7.0). The determinism note and the paper 3 station image use the same vLLM build.
- Batch-invariant mode: the unit harness cannot set container environment variables, so `Dockerfile.bi`
  adds one layer, `ENV VLLM_BATCH_INVARIANT=1`, and nothing else.
  - It is built on each host with `SOURCE_DATE_EPOCH=0`, without provenance or SBOM attestations.
  - The local image ID is `sha256:c92aec7952df6db14f57b021b8fe6bd91ff80919debfeeb14d2fc304e8549318` on
    every host (checked 2026-11-28).
  - Units pin that ID. The analysis checks each container's image ID and environment against it.
- Flags for every batch-invariant unit except the V1-deployment arm:
  `--dtype bfloat16 --max-model-len 8192 --seed 0 --max-num-batched-tokens 8192 --max-num-seqs 1024
  --attention-backend FLASH_ATTN --gpu-memory-utilization 0.25 --kv-cache-memory-bytes 17179869184`.
  - The KV pool is fixed at 16 GiB on both devices, so the scheduler has the same number of KV blocks on
    each. With a fixed pool, vLLM uses `--gpu-memory-utilization` only for its start-up free-memory check.
    0.25 lets that check pass on the shared Spark.
  - `--max-num-batched-tokens 8192 --max-num-seqs 1024` equal vLLM's own default for both devices.
  - FLASH_ATTN is the first backend vLLM tries on compute capability 12.x and supports batch invariance.
    Pinning it rules out different backends on the two chips. If it fails to start on either device, the
    unit fails and its model is INCOMPLETE.
  - Prefix caching, chunked prefill and CUDA graphs stay at vLLM's defaults, as V1 and R1 serve.
- V1-deployment arm (RTX PRO 6000 only): the same flags without `--kv-cache-memory-bytes`, and with
  `--gpu-memory-utilization 0.85`, as V1 serves today.
- Control arm (RTX PRO 6000 only): the base image without the ENV layer, so batch-invariant mode is off.
  The flags are the same as the primary arms.
- Served model names: `qwen3-1.7b`, `granite-4.1-3b`, `granite-4.1-8b`. Each unit runs its own server on
  loopback. No production or research router is used.

## Analysis (frozen in `analyze.py`)

Two outputs are identical if both are strings and byte-equal in UTF-8, matched by item id. There is no
tolerance: one differing byte makes a pair DIFFERENT.

A unit passes its checks only if all of the following hold:
- it has a record for each of the 1,179 ids and no other ids, and no id has two different outputs;
- every record has a string output, a finish reason of `stop` or `length`, and integer prompt and output
  token counts;
- every attempt that produced records has a treatment record, and every treatment record matches the unit's
  row in the Units table:
  - unit label, concurrency and `aarch64`;
  - GPU name, driver and compute capability exactly as in "Hardware and platform", and a GPU UUID;
  - image ID, and the exact list of `VLLM_*` environment variables of that image (the base image's six, plus
    `VLLM_BATCH_INVARIANT=1` for batch-invariant units);
  - serving arguments exactly as frozen, and the served model name;
- its server log exists, shows FLASH_ATTN as the only attention backend, one GPU KV cache size, and
  running-request counts.

All units of one model on one hardware class must report one and the same GPU UUID, or the model is
INCOMPLETE. Exact duplicate records keep the first and are counted. Lines that do not parse are counted and
skipped.

A comparison is INCOMPLETE if any of these holds:
- either unit fails its checks or any item is missing;
- any item's prompt token count differs between the two units (a sign of tokenizer or template drift);
- both units use the fixed KV pool and their logged KV cache sizes differ;
- for a batch or control comparison, the lower-concurrency unit's peak running requests are not below the
  other unit's (the server batches did not differ);
- for the hardware comparison, the two units' running-request histograms (bins of 16, idle samples with
  no running request left out) share no bin (the two devices never ran comparable batches).

Otherwise it is IDENTICAL (1,179/1,179) or DIFFERENT.

Comparisons per model:
- hardware: m-spark-c128 vs m-6000-c128;
- determinism_spark: m-spark-c128 vs m-spark-c128-r2;
- determinism_6000: m-6000-c128 vs m-6000-c128-r2;
- batch_spark: m-spark-c128 vs m-spark-c64;
- batch_6000: m-6000-c128 vs m-6000-c32;
- control_bi_off: m-6000-nobi-c128 vs m-6000-nobi-c32.

Verdict per model, the first rule that applies:
1. INCOMPLETE if any of the six comparisons is INCOMPLETE, or the same-card check fails.
2. NONDETERMINISTIC if either determinism comparison is DIFFERENT.
3. BATCH-VARIANT if either batch comparison is DIFFERENT.
4. HARDWARE-DIFFERENT if hardware is DIFFERENT.
5. UNINFORMATIVE if control_bi_off is IDENTICAL (the five comparisons above are then all IDENTICAL).
6. PASS otherwise (the five are IDENTICAL and the control is DIFFERENT).

All six comparison results are reported whatever the verdict.

Reported, not part of the verdict:
- kv_pool_6000: m-6000-c128 vs m-6000-gmu085-c128, a single unreplicated run of V1's current flags;
- hardware at unequal lower concurrencies: m-spark-c64 vs m-6000-c32 (Spark 64, RTX PRO 6000 32);
- hardware between the replicates: m-spark-c128-r2 vs m-6000-c128-r2.

## Procedure

1. Before queueing, on each host: confirm the serving image ID and every file hash above.
2. The queue runs each unit under its own preemptible fleet claim.
   - Spark units refuse to start below 48 GiB of available memory.
   - RTX PRO 6000 units refuse to start below 84 GiB free on the card.
3. Units run in any order and may be interrupted and resumed; each item records its attempt number. A reply
   that fails the record schema is not written and is requested again on resume.
4. After all units finish, run `score.py` once per unit. Then run `analyze.py` once, with all 27 units, the
   two reference runs and all 27 score files. Verdicts are read from `analysis.json`.

## Stop rules

- Each unit runs to completion, with no early stop on results. The Granite units run whatever the Qwen3
  verdict is.
- A unit whose job fails (any item still failing after 6 retries) is resumed once. If it fails again, it
  stops, and its model's verdict is INCOMPLETE.
- A completed unit is never rerun to resolve a mismatch. A mismatch is the result.
- The whole study stops on any of these:
  - any eviction of another session's model;
  - any call to a production or research router;
  - Spark memory below the floor while a unit runs;
  - a serving-image ID or model-file hash that differs from this card on any host.
- If a hardware class reaches its budget cap, its remaining units do not run. Models whose units all
  finished are still judged. The others are INCOMPLETE.

## Budget, kill criteria and interruption contract

- Estimate, unit by unit (generation minutes; cold start adds about 6 per Spark unit and 4 per RTX PRO 6000
  unit):
  - Spark: Qwen3-1.7B 7 per c128 unit and 10 at c64; Granite 3B 14 and 20; Granite 8B 33 and 46. Total about
    184 minutes of generation and 54 of cold start, about 4.0 GPU-hours. The c128 figures scale the earlier
    408 s for Qwen3-1.7B by model size.
  - RTX PRO 6000: Qwen3-1.7B 3 per c128 unit and 6 per c32 unit; Granite 3B 5 and 10; Granite 8B 10 and 20.
    Total about 144 minutes of generation and 72 of cold start, about 3.6 GPU-hours. These assume about
    three times the earlier GB300 time of 64 s for Qwen3-1.7B. They are rough.
- Caps: 5.0 GPU-hours on the Spark and 5.0 on the RTX PRO 6000.
- Kill criteria: the study-wide stop rules above.
- Interruption contract: maximum release 5 minutes; maximum lost work 45 minutes, the job's per-request
  timeout of 2,700 s, since requests in flight at an interruption are lost (a 4,096-token Qwen3-1.7B reply
  took up to 181 s on the Spark before, and Granite 8B is about five times larger); the claim is revocable.

## Deviations

Any operational change after this file is public (a host, a card, a retry) is recorded as a dated amendment
in this directory, before the units it affects run. Nothing is fixed silently. Once any unit has finished,
no amendment may change the units, flags, comparisons, checks or verdict rules; such a change needs a new
protocol version.

## Outcome

Verdicts, counts and every number in the write-up will be recorded in `outcome-<date>.md` in this
directory, with a map from each number to its receipt file and line.
