## Preregistered outcome

Primary (LiveCodeBench, difficulty-adjusted, adjacent quarters): **not comparable** under the three-point rule. The mean absolute adjusted drift was 6.04 points (95% CI [5.25, 8.78], n=196 model-pair cells, 28 models, 1,043 problems).

Secondary (LiveBench): coding and language release pairs were classified not comparable. The instruction-following pair was not analyzed (too few task-matched questions). The other categories had a single release in the snapshot.

## Caveats to read with the result (found after the run, recorded before PI review)

1. **The interval method is biased for this statistic.** In two reported statistics the point estimate lies outside its own 95% percentile-bootstrap interval: median Kendall tau-b 0.850 against CI [0.74, 0.83] (n=7 quarter pairs), and LiveBench coding drift 4.62 against CI [5.71, 13.68] (n=154 models). Resampling items inflates a mean of absolute differences and shrinks a median of rank correlations. The preregistered percentile intervals are therefore not reliable coverage statements for these quantities. The primary interval is shifted upward by the same mechanism.
2. **The statistic counts sampling noise as drift.** With 73 to 170 problems per included quarter, a model's window mean has a standard error of several points. The absolute window-to-window difference therefore exceeds three points even with no systematic change. The result supports the narrow reading the protocol defines: a model's score on one quarter's problems routinely differs from its score on the next quarter's by more than three points. It does not show that the benchmark or the models changed systematically over time.
3. The exploratory post-minus-pre release-marker gaps are large and negative for every model with a non-placeholder marker. They are confounded with problem-date trends and are not a contamination finding.

No analysis was rerun or altered. A corrected design (noise-calibrated drift, for example observed absolute drift minus its null expectation under within-window resampling, and a bias-corrected interval) would need a new preregistered version. Whether this outcome is a meaningful null worth a post is the PI's call.
