## Preregistered outcome

- **Supported.** CIG minus SFT, MATH-500 Avg@16: +23.69 points (paired bootstrap 95% CI [21.29, 26.11], n=500 problems). The paper claims +26.9; the preregistered bar was at least +13.45 with a CI lower bound above 0.
- Pass@16 difference: +6.40 points (95% CI [3.00, 9.80], n=500 problems).
- Baseline reproduction: our SFT arm scored 42.3 Avg@16 (n=500) against the paper's 41.5, so it is "faithful" (within 5 points). CIG scored 66.0 (n=500) against the paper's 68.4.
- Harness check: the base model scored 46.3 Avg@16 (n=500) against the paper's 39.0, inside the 10-point flag threshold. Pass@16 was 87.0 (n=500) against 86.0.
- Truncation at the length cap: base 2.9%, SFT 0.1%, CIG 7.5% of 8,000 samples each, all under the 20% kill threshold.

## Exploratory (not preregistered tests; same bootstrap, seed 42)

- SFT minus base Avg@16: -3.99 points (95% CI [-5.95, -2.04], n=500 problems). With the authors' recipe, standard SFT makes the model worse than no training on this benchmark, matching the paper's own below-base SFT aggregate.
- CIG minus base Avg@16: +19.70 points (95% CI [17.59, 21.80], n=500 problems).

## What this establishes and what it does not

The authors' released code and recipe reproduce a gain of close to the claimed size over their own SFT setting, at one seed, on MATH-500, with a fixed prompt and grader.

It does not settle whether the gain is over a fair baseline. Under this recipe, standard SFT scores below the untrained model, so part of the headline "gain over SFT" is recovery from a baseline that hurts. A separately tuned SFT baseline (for example, learning rate or prompt-template choices) was out of scope and is the obvious next control. Other seeds, the other five benchmarks, the larger model and the code results are untested here.

Observation: the trainer ran 234 optimizer steps where the paper reports 233 (29,830 rows at a global batch of 128 leave a final partial batch).
